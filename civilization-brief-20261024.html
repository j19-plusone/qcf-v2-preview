思考人类：人类健康数据主权与公平价值分享

Jessica Sage · QCF 发起人｜数字文明架构师

当人工智能开始从海量健康数据中学习，真正需要回答的并不只是模型能做什么，而是：谁有权决定个人健康数据的用途？谁承担数据滥用的风险？谁能分享由此产生的公共利益？

一、健康数据是一种特殊的信任关系

健康信息不仅描述一个人当下的身体状态，还可能涉及遗传特征、家族关联、生活习惯及未来疾病风险。它可能帮助科研人员发现疾病规律，却也可能在未经充分理解和同意的情况下被重复使用。因此，数据采集的合法性只是起点，长期治理同样重要。

二、从数据获取走向数据主权

真正有意义的健康数据主权，应当包括清晰的知情同意、具体用途限制、访问记录、可撤销的未来授权，以及对二次使用的监督。技术可以提供加密、权限管理和审计机制；但研究成果的留存、跨境数据流动和撤回权的边界，仍需要法律与伦理制度共同界定。

三、公平分享科研成果的价值

开放获取研究文章《Learning from Nigeria’s Public-Private Pathways to Generate Inclusive Healthcare Data》以尼日利亚的医疗合作经验为切入点，探讨如何通过公共部门、企业和医疗机构的合作，改善医疗服务与数据代表性。它提醒我们：数据提供者及其所在社区不应只是研究材料的来源，还应被纳入治理和利益分享的讨论。

这篇研究评论并未证明某一合作模式已经普遍成功。部分项目仍需要独立评估。因此，我们更应把它作为设计治理原则的参考，而不是现成的全球解决方案。

四、QCF的文明共建思考

未来的文明身份不应成为收集个人DNA或健康数据的理由。独一无二的生命值得尊重，但DNA本身不应被等同于公开身份标识，也不应在缺乏充分保障时被作为认证凭据。任何涉及遗传数据的研究，都应坚持自愿参与、数据最小化、用途限定与可审计授权。

对QCF而言，值得推动的是一套以人为中心的研究原则：让科技服务生命，让贡献得到尊重，让数据价值与公共利益之间建立透明、可信的联系。

Rethinking Humanity: Human Health Data Sovereignty & Equitable Benefit Sharing

Jessica Sage · QCF Founder | Digital Civilization Architect

As artificial intelligence learns from increasingly extensive health datasets, the question is not merely what a model can accomplish. Who determines how personal health data is used? Who bears the risks of misuse? And who shares in the public value created?

1. Health data is a relationship of trust

Health information can reveal far more than a person's present condition. It may concern genetic characteristics, family relationships, everyday behavior and future health risks. Such information can advance research, yet may also be reused beyond what people originally understood or authorized. Lawful collection is only the beginning; long-term governance matters.

2. From access to sovereignty

Meaningful health data sovereignty calls for informed consent, purpose limitations, access records, the ability to revoke future permissions, and oversight of secondary uses. Encryption, access controls and audit trails can help, but the limits of withdrawal, research retention and cross-border transfers require legal and ethical governance as well.

3. Sharing the value of research fairly

The open-access article Learning from Nigeria’s Public-Private Pathways to Generate Inclusive Healthcare Data examines healthcare collaborations in Nigeria and asks how partnerships among public institutions, companies and healthcare providers can improve both care and data representation. It prompts a broader principle: people and communities contributing data should not be treated solely as inputs to research; their interests belong in governance and benefit-sharing discussions.

The article does not establish that any one partnership model has been proven universally successful. Some reported outcomes still require independent evaluation. Its value lies in the governance questions it raises, not in a ready-made global blueprint.

4. A QCF perspective on co-building civilization

A future Civilization ID should never become a pretext for collecting DNA or personal health information. Every person's biology is distinctive and deserves respect, but DNA must not be treated as a public identity number or an inherently safe authentication credential. Genetic research must remain voluntary, proportionate, purpose-limited and accountable.

QCF's opportunity is to advance human-centered principles: technology in service of life, recognition of contributions, and transparent connections between data-derived value and the public good.
